THE PROBLEM

cases-run is what executed, and it depends on the SHAPE of the target. An It
with -ForEach over the public functions produces seven cases against a module
with seven commands and two against a module with two. Runs 002 and 003 are
two builds of the SAME module and reported 57 and 55: the denominator moved
underneath the score, so 57/57 and 39/55 are not two points on one scale.

cases-defined is read from the suite SOURCE by parsing it - the count of It
statements the selected tags select. The target is never consulted: no file of
it is read, no -ForEach is expanded, nothing is discovered. That is the whole
property, and it is why the number cannot move with the target.

Read from the AST, not with a regex, for the reason the suite gives about the
build file: a block comment quoting an It is not an It.

NO ASSERTION WAS WEAKENED. Nothing is skipped, and cases-run and the score are
computed exactly as before. This adds a denominator that holds still.

THE CONTROL - it must move with the SELECTION, or it measures nothing

A number that never changed would be stable and useless. Against run-002:

  -Tag Universal                                   cases-defined  9   cases-run 17
  -Tag Universal,HouseStyle                        cases-defined 23   cases-run 41
  -Tag Universal,Repository,HouseStyle,RequiresBuild cases-defined 33  cases-run 57

It tracks the tags the caller selected and nothing about the target. Both
halves are needed: invariant across targets, responsive to selection.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE COMPARISON RULE FOR RUNS 004-006

  COMPARE ON cases-defined. REPORT cases-run BESIDE IT. Never compare two
  runs on cases-run alone.

  A run states four numbers: passed, cases-run, cases-defined, and the score.
  Two runs are comparable when their cases-defined and their tag selection
  agree. When their cases-run differs, that difference is a fact about the
  two TARGETS - one has more public commands, or fewer files a -ForEach
  enumerates - and it is reported, never averaged away.

  A change in cases-defined between two runs means THE SUITE CHANGED. That is
  a harness event and needs saying out loud, because it silently rescales
  every score before and after it. Runs 004-006 are meant to be compared to
  run 003, so the suite must not change under them; if it must, the runs that
  straddle the change are not comparable and the record says so.

  ScorePct is unchanged and still divides by cases-run, deliberately: it
  answers "what fraction of what applied to this target passed", which is a
  fair question about one target. cases-defined answers the different
  question of whether two runs were graded against the same suite at all.
