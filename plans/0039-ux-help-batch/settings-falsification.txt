Row 4 is the control that matters and is the one a hand-rolled settings
reader omits. '90' as a STRING is a near miss on a known key - the key is
spelled correctly and the value looks right - and PowerShell would coerce it
to 90 without complaint. A reader that coerces has decided what the user
meant. Refusing names the key and the expected shape instead.

Row 6 applies the same rule to the caller. A typo in a script that invokes
the grader is not more acceptable than a typo in a user's file, and a reader
that validates one input and trusts the other has a hole in exactly the
place its own tests do not look.
