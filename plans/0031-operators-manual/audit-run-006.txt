  READ THAT LAST ROW CAREFULLY - it is the second place this audit nearly
  reported a finding that was not one. There is no '12' anywhere in the report.
  I went looking for a CasesPassed field, found 'cases: []', and my first
  instinct was that the 12/12 was unverifiable from the artifact.

  It is not. Reading evals/functional/Compare-Graph.ps1 settles it: the
  report's 'cases' array is the variable the comparator calls affectedCases -
  the list of declared cases that a difference touched. An EMPTY array means no
  case was affected, which is what 12 of 12 passing looks like in this format.
  The score is recorded as an absence, and 'agree: true, differenceCount: 0' is
  the same claim said twice.

  Two rules come out of that, and they are why this step is in the manual.
  First: when an artifact does not contain the number you expected, read the
  code that WRITES the artifact before concluding the number is missing.
  Second: an audit that had stopped at 'cases is empty, so the claim is
  unsupported' would have manufactured a finding out of its own ignorance of
  the format - which is worse than not auditing, because it looks like rigour.
